What sets UNOCTA apart

Most imaging software grew up at a desk beside the telescope and learned to run unattended later. UNOCTA started from a roof nobody can reach and works backwards from what must stay true when nobody is watching. Nine things it does that the usual tools leave to a plugin, a script or a person.

01

Unattended is the baseline, not a mode

The night supervisor owns dusk to dawn: open, prepare, image, recover, close. Nothing waits for a click. A path that would wait for a person is treated as a defect, not a feature.

UsuallyAutomation layered over an operator's workflow. A prompt or a stuck state waits for someone at the desk.
UNOCTANo manual start. Every wait has a deadline and a fallback. A person is an optional observer.
02

Several trains on one mount

A rig is a mount with the trains it carries. Trains share slews, dithers and meridian flips through barriers and fail on their own: one train's hung driver does not end the other's night.

UsuallyOne imaging train per profile. A second OTA means a second instance and coordination done by hand.
UNOCTASite, rig and train are the model from the start, with a plan lane and progress per train.
03

Knows how the observatory is wired

Which USB port and which outlet feed each device, the order to power up and down, the path that brings a device back. Automation addresses a role; only Core resolves it to a device or a channel.

UsuallyA device list. The wiring lives in the operator's memory, a checklist or a script.
UNOCTARecovery power-cycles the one device that hung and checks that the right one came back.
04

Self-healing, on a bounded ladder

Retry, recover (reconnect, USB reset, power cycle), degrade, defer, escalate with a deadline, then the safe state. Each tier has a limit. The night survives a restart and picks up mid-run from its checkpoint.

UsuallyAn endless retry, a dialog nobody sees, or a silent stop until morning.
UNOCTABounded, explicit, logged. A notification can speed up a choice; it never gates the fallback.
05

Safety is its own authority

Core decides, fail-closed: unknown, stale or contradictory input is unsafe. Nothing above it can override a limit, and it keeps protecting the hardware when the planner, the console and the network are gone. The UPS is a safety source with a measured runtime budget.

UsuallyThe sequence parks and closes, provided the sequence is still running.
UNOCTAA fixed response ladder, max age and debounce per source, and a lockout that is shown, never silent.
06

A plate solver of its own

A near solver against Gaia DR3, built for the small field of a long focal length where the common solvers give up. No index files: catalogue tiles are fetched once and cached, and the next run's stars are ready before it starts.

UsuallyAn external solver and its index files, which lose the field below a certain size.
UNOCTA1.3 s per solve at 0.27″/px. The familiar solvers sit behind it in a chain, not instead of it.
07

Autofocus from a slope, on known stars

A slope model learned from past runs focuses from a few frames on one wing; the V-curve is the fallback, not the routine. The first frame is solved and the stars to follow are chosen from Gaia, so every frame measures the same real stars.

UsuallyA full V-curve every time, measured on whatever bright blobs the frame offers.
UNOCTAHot pixels, galaxy cores and blended pairs are set aside by name. A refocus that made the stars wider is undone.
08

Sees the cloud the sky sensor misses

The calibrated allsky becomes a transparency map: catalogue stars are looked for where they should be, and how much they are dimmed tells thin cloud from thick. The rig's own frames pause a run when their star count falls against the target's baseline.

UsuallyOne infrared sky sensor. Thin high cloud reads as clear while the frames go to waste.
UNOCTATransparency per sky cell, drawn on the live frame. The roof stays a decision of the safety sources.
09

Core, the night supervisor, the planner and the notifier in one process on the observatory PC, serving its own console over REST and WebSocket. Close the tab, lose the link, and the night goes on. No cloud, no account.

UsuallyA remote desktop into the observatory PC, and a night that depends on that session.
UNOCTAWindows, macOS, Linux or a Raspberry Pi. Any browser, on your own private network.

Autofocus on known stars

A slope model learned from past runs focuses from a few frames on one wing; a V-curve scan when no model is ready or the frames do not fit. The first frame is solved and the stars to follow are chosen from the Gaia catalogue, so every frame of the run measures the same known stars, the way a person would follow them.

  • A hot pixel, a galaxy's core or a knot of nebula has no catalogue star and is not followed.
  • A star with a neighbour that would share its aperture once defocused is set aside, as is one saturated or cut by the edge. The note names each.
  • HFR and FWHM on every star and every frame, eccentricity and its angle beside them.
  • A refocus is triggered by a sustained HFR rise above the frames' own scatter, and an autofocus that made the stars wider is undone.

A solver of its own, for long focal lengths

ASTAP gives up on the small field of a long focal length, so Core has its own near solver against Gaia DR3. It reads only the frame's pixels and a hint: where the mount says it points, and the scale from the camera and the OTA. Rotation, mirroring and skew are found; the scale may be 5 % off, the hint up to 10°. A hint that is wrong fails, never a wrong placement.

  • Every run frame, autofocus frame and centring is solved, with pointing error, rotation, scale and time in a running table.
  • No index files. Gaia tiles are fetched once, cached, and can be downloaded ahead to a magnitude, so a field not yet seen is ready offline. The next run's stars are fetched before it starts.
  • A chain, not a choice: Gaia first, ASTAP, astrometry.net and PlateSolve 2 behind it, each with its mode and timeout.
  • Solve now from the console, and sync the mount if you want.

Safety is fail-closed

Core decides. Unknown, stale or contradictory input is unsafe. Nothing above Core, not the planner, not the console, not a notification, can override a limit, and Core keeps protecting the hardware when all of them are gone.

One decision, with its sources and its rules

Every safety source is counted or advisory, with a max age and a debounce. The unsafe response is a fixed ladder. Reopening waits until it has been safe for a while, closures per night are limited, and a lockout is shown, never silent. Dry-run any source to see the decision and the commands it would refuse.

UPS as a safety source, reserve as a budget

The UPS joins the safety sources, and its runtime is checked against a budget measured from real power-down sequences, margin included. On battery: power down what is not imaging, stop new exposures, park and close, warm and power off the cameras if the runtime covers it, request the host shutdown. Notifications

A phone that hears the night

One Pushover channel, off until you switch it on. Four tiers (critical, intervention, warning, info), each mapped to a Pushover priority or off. Unsafe with the roof not closed, a safe state and an open escalation reach the phone; a message never carries an id. A test button, the last deliveries, and a bounded queue. A night completes whether or not anyone reads a message.

Device actions

Motors on, motors off

Record a driver's own actions, such as a mount's MotorOn and MotorOff, with their parameters, and name one as the device's enable action. Core sends it before a park or unpark, under its own hold, and logs a device.enable event. Manual control runs any listed action with the same audit trail.

Rig policies

Moments in the night

Attach actions to moments: motors on at night start, an autofocus after the meridian flip, motors off at night end. Each step says whether it may fail, so a step that does not matter cannot stop the night and one that does, will.

Server and client, cleanly apart

The console runs in any browser, signed in with a bearer token, and only requests actions. It is never an authority: nothing in it, and nothing above Core, can override a limit. The server decides where it listens: this computer only, every network, or chosen interfaces, on a port you pick. It is never forwarded to the internet; the supported remote path is your own WireGuard or Tailscale network.

  • Alpaca over HTTP on any OS, ASCOM on Windows, NUT and USB-HID for the UPS, and a simulator, all behind one per-class device contract with one shared fixture suite.
  • The console is bundled, so there is no web server to keep alive. The console keeps Core's time, not the browser's.
  • The observatory's link carries only Core's state and the frame tiles in view, lossless PNG, cached by the browser; the sky imagery behind the framing view is the browser's own download from CDS.

Users, roles, and an admin that cannot vanish

A user directory on the server, passwords hashed with PBKDF2 and never written in clear, no default once the first admin has set their own. Three roles, each a fixed set of permissions: an observer sees everything and changes nothing, an operator drives the rig and orders the night, an administrator configures the observatory and its people. Nobody, in any role, bypasses a Core interlock.

  • A changed role, a reset password or a deleted account ends that user's sessions at once.
  • The last administrator cannot be deleted or demoted, and nobody changes their own role.
  • Ten failed sign-ins for one name refuse it for a minute.
  • Every user changes their own password from their account; every access decision is in the Logbook.

Simulator

A whole observatory, simulated

Every device class as a simulator with a fast-forward clock and a rendered Gaia sky, so a night's plan, a cloud event, a driver hang and a mid-night process kill can be tried before dusk. The demo observatory profile ships with ten targets and a night of runs, and its clock can be moved from the top bar, so it shows a night by day. Every screenshot on this page is from it.

Checkpoints

Kill the process, keep the night

The night supervisor and every run checkpoint durably; a restart mid-night reconciles against the devices and carries on, and records an interrupted run once. Sessions end at a restart; profiles, the user directory and every event survive it.

Evidence

Every decision with its reason

Commands, access decisions and domain events go to a durable event store; an automatic action records its inputs, its policy and its result. The Logbook reads it live, the Experience page learns from it, and the morning can read it later.

What comes next

The order is fixed: autonomy before breadth. The milestone that matters is not "it can image" but "it imaged all night and nobody was awake". Foundations, Core, safety, power, environment, the Orchestrator and the night supervisor are built. This is what is open, in order; where a part of it is already in the console, it is marked built.
